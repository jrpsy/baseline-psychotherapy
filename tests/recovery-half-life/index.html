Recovery Half-Life Check

A reflection tool created by J.R. Hernandez, psychotherapist at Baseline Psychotherapy. Not a psychometric test, and it doesn't pretend to be: for validated instruments, see the screeners.

Recovery has a duration. After a demanding day, a hard week, or a real vacation, your system takes a certain amount of time to return to its baseline: to feeling like you. This check looks at one thing only: whether that duration is stable, stretching, or no longer completing. Eight questions, two minutes. This is a reflection tool from this practice, not a validated clinical instrument: for validated numbers, the CBI and WHO-5 are one click away.

Half-Life Check FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About
the Recovery Half-Life Check

Recovery half-life is a working concept from this practice, taken from the founder's writing on burnout: the time your system needs after a demand to return to its baseline, to feeling like yourself. The image is borrowed from physics: not how much energy you have, but how long the return takes. When that duration stretches, it is often the earliest visible signal that regulation is under strain.

It is a reflection tool, not psychometrics. A validated burnout instrument such as the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (CBI) has been tested against large samples and gives you a scored result with known properties. This check has not been validated: it asks eight honest questions about one variable, recovery time, and returns a reading, not a score. Use it to notice a pattern, and use the CBI for the number.

First, put a number on it: the CBI and the WHO-5 take a few minutes and are validated. Second, treat the trend seriously rather than the label: a stretching recovery is the stage where structured changes to load, sleep, and boundaries work best; a recovery that no longer completes usually needs more than rest. Talking to a professional, here or anywhere, is a reasonable next step in both cases.
