The Protocol

What the Protocol is

The Baseline Psychotherapy Protocol is a structured sequence used in this practice to examine a single reaction: one situation, one activation, one episode at a time. It is not the whole of the practice, and it is not a school of therapy. It is a base tool: the kind of instrument a clinician reaches for again and again because it makes the invisible part of a reaction observable.

The premise is simple to state. Between what happens and what you do, there is a system: the body responds, the mind builds a story, an impulse to stop feeling something appears, and behavior follows. Most of this runs automatically. The Protocol slows that chain down and makes each link visible, in order.

I built it by integrating the approaches that hold the strongest evidence for this kind of work: cognitive-behavioral therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy, emotional intelligence training, and somatic, body-first awareness. Each step of the sequence comes from one of those traditions; the integration, the order, and the clinical logic that binds them are mine.

The clinical map

The map it works with

Stimulus→Processing and appraisal→Physiological response→The story→The regulatory impulse→Behavior

This is a clinical map, not a diagram of the brain. Its purpose is practical: when a reaction can be laid out on the map, it stops being a single automatic unit and becomes a set of parts that can be examined one by one. Which part started it. What the body did. What the mind said it meant. What the urge to fix it demanded. And what was actually true.

Origin

Where it comes from

I created the Protocol in session, not at a desk. Over years of clinical work with professionals, expats, and couples, the same combination kept proving itself: a CBT-grounded reading of the story the mind builds, ACT's defusion and values work, the emotional granularity that emotional intelligence training develops, and the body-first attention of somatic approaches. I refined the sequence because clients used it between sessions and reported back what held and what did not. The book version exists because they kept asking for the tool in a form they could keep.

In session

The Protocol and therapy

In session, the Protocol is applied with clinical guidance: the steps are the same ones the book teaches, but a therapist calibrates them to your history, keeps the work honest when the mind negotiates, and connects what the tool reveals to a treatment plan. The book is complete on its own for self-practice. Therapy is where the tool meets a clinician.
